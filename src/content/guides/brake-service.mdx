The Metacycle has separate front and rear hydraulic disc brakes, and each has its own fluid reservoir with a sightglass on the handlebars. As on a scooter, the right lever works the front brake and the left lever works the rear; there's no foot brake. Sondors' owner's manual gives the fluid type, the replacement interval and the wear limits, but no step-by-step procedure (it says to use a Sondors technician, and there aren't any now). The fluid change below is the standard method for any single-caliper motorcycle brake. Nobody has confirmed it on a Metacycle yet, which is why this guide is marked _theory_. If you've done it, tell us what worked.

**From the owner's manual:**

- **Fluid:** DOT 3 or DOT 4 only, from a sealed bottle. Fluid from an opened bottle, or fluid bled out of the bike, has absorbed moisture and must not be reused.
- **Replace the fluid every 12 months,** however far you've ridden.
- **Pads:** replace when less than 3 mm (0.1 in) of friction material is left.
- **Rotors:** replace if thinner than 2 mm or visibly damaged.
- **Lever free travel:** 2–3 mm.

### Check fluid and wear (every ride, and monthly)

<Step n={1}>
  With the bike upright on level ground, look at both sightglasses on the handlebars. The fluid
  should be above the MIN mark. The level drops a little as the pads wear, which is normal. A sudden
  or repeated drop means a leak, so find it before riding.
</Step>

<Step n={2}>
  Look at each caliper and check how much pad material is left. Replace the pads if there's less
  than 3 mm. Check the rotors for scoring, cracks or warping.
</Step>

<Step n={3}>
  Squeeze each lever. It should have 2–3 mm of free travel and then firm up. A spongy lever means
  air in the line or old fluid; bleed it (below).
</Step>

### Replace the brake fluid (every 12 months)

Do one brake at a time. A full change is an "extended bleed": push fresh fluid through until clean, bubble-free fluid comes out of the caliper.

<Step n={4}>
  Cover the tank, display and paint around the reservoir with rags. Turn the handlebar so the
  reservoir you're working on sits level. TODO: what screws hold the reservoir caps, and their
  driver size.
</Step>

<Step n={5}>
  Remove the reservoir cap and the rubber diaphragm under it. Suck most of the old fluid out with a
  syringe or turkey baster, but don't let it run dry, or you'll pull air into the master cylinder.
  Refill with fresh DOT 4 (or DOT 3) to just below the top.
</Step>

<Step n={6}>
  At the caliper, pull the rubber cap off the bleed screw. Fit a clear hose over it with the other
  end in a catch bottle holding a little fresh fluid, so air can't be sucked back up. TODO: the
  bleed-screw wrench size for the front and rear calipers.
</Step>

<Step n={7}>
  Squeeze the lever and hold it. Open the bleed screw about a quarter turn, let the fluid and air
  flow out, then close the screw **before** you release the lever. Repeat. Keep topping up the
  reservoir so it never drops below MIN.
</Step>

<Step n={8}>
  Keep going until the fluid in the hose is clean and has no bubbles. Old fluid is darker; new fluid
  is clear to light amber. Expect to run a reservoir's worth through, or a little more. TODO: how
  much fluid each brake takes for a full change.
</Step>

<Step n={9}>
  Tighten the bleed screw snugly. Don't overtighten it; the threads are small. Refit the dust cap.
  TODO: bleed-screw torque.
</Step>

<Step n={10}>
  Set the fluid level between MIN and MAX, refit the diaphragm and cap, and wipe everything down.
  Pump the lever until it's firm. Repeat steps 4–10 for the other brake.
</Step>

<Step n={11}>
  If the lever stays spongy, let the bike sit for a while with the cap off and the reservoir level,
  then bleed again. If you still can't get a firm lever, don't ride it.
</Step>

### Replace the pads

<Step n={12}>
  Rear pads are **EBC FA266**. Several compounds share that number, and owners report the organic
  ones are the quietest. TODO: the front pad part number and the caliper model aren't known. If
  you've changed your front pads, send us the part number, or photos of the old pad and the caliper.
</Step>

<Step n={13}>
  Remove the pad retaining pin(s) and the old pads. Push the pistons back into the caliper with
  steady, even pressure. The fluid level in the reservoir rises as you do, so watch it doesn't
  overflow. TODO: whether the caliper has to come off to change the pads, and the caliper bolt size
  and torque.
</Step>

<Step n={14}>
  Fit the new pads and the retaining pin(s). Pump the lever until it's firm **before** you move the
  bike; the first squeezes do nothing while the pistons come back out.
</Step>

<Step n={15}>
  Bed in the new pads: make several moderate stops from about 20–30 mph on a quiet road, without
  locking the wheel. Braking will be weaker until the pads have bedded in.
</Step>
